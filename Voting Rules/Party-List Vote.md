# Party-List Vote

**Party-list voting** is a multi-winner, proportional system in which voters cast a ballot for a political party (or sometimes for candidates within a party). Seats are allocated to parties in proportion to their vote share, then filled from each party's list of candidates.

## How it works
1. Each party presents an ordered (closed) or unordered (open) list of candidates.
2. Voters vote for a party (and, in open-list systems, for candidates within it).
3. Seats are apportioned to parties by their vote share using a divisor or quota method (e.g., D'Hondt, Sainte-Laguë).
4. Seats are filled from each party's list.

## Key characteristics
- **Closed list:** party fixes the order of candidates. **Open list:** voters influence which candidates are elected.
- Produces highly proportional outcomes; common in continental Europe and many democracies.
- Often paired with single-member districts in *mixed-member* systems.
