# Positional

**Positional** voting rules assign points to candidates based on their *rank position* on each ballot, then elect the candidate(s) with the most points. Different point schedules define different rules.

## How it works
- Voters rank candidates.
- Each rank position is worth a fixed number of points (e.g., in the **Borda count** with *n* candidates: *n*−1 for 1st, *n*−2 for 2nd, …, 0 for last).
- Points are summed across all ballots; highest total wins.

## Key characteristics
- A family of rules: [[Plurality]] (points only to 1st place) and anti-plurality are special cases; the Borda count is the best-known general example.
- Considers the full ranking, rewarding broadly acceptable candidates.
- Vulnerable to strategic ranking and to manipulation by adding/removing candidates.
