# Limited

**Limited voting** is a multi-winner rule in which each voter has *fewer votes than there are seats*, and (unlike [[Cumulative]] voting) may give no more than one vote to any candidate. The top vote-getters fill the seats.

## How it works
- For *k* seats, each voter gets some number of votes *v* where *v* < *k*.
- A voter may cast at most one vote per candidate.
- The *k* candidates with the most votes win.

## Key characteristics
- By limiting votes below the number of seats, it gives minority groups a realistic chance at representation (semi-proportional).
- The extreme case **v = 1** is the **single non-transferable vote (SNTV)**, used historically in Japan and Taiwan.
- Encourages parties to nominate carefully and voters to coordinate.

## Related
- [[Cumulative]] — voters get votes equal to seats and may concentrate them.
- [[Block Plurality]] — voters get votes equal to seats (one per candidate).
