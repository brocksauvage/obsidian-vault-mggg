Behavioral ranking models within social choice theory are a way of modeling how individual voters rank candidates based on their [[Preference Profile|preference profiles]]. In these models, assumptions are made about how voters behave, their inclinations to vote for slates of candidates, and their propensity for crossover. As such, they are probabilistic - we can only indicate the likelihood of how voters will rank candidates on their ballot.

### Plackett-Luce (The "Impulsive" Voter)

This model assumes that a voter will rank candidates impulsively, with a weight or "worth" assigned to each candidate.
