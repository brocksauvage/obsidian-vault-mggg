# Ranked Choice Voting

**Ranked Choice Voting (RCV)**, in its single-winner form (also called *instant-runoff voting*), lets voters rank candidates in order of preference. If no candidate has a majority of first-choice votes, the last-place candidate is eliminated and their ballots transfer to each voter's next ranked choice. The process repeats until a candidate has a majority.

## How it works
1. Voters rank candidates (1st, 2nd, 3rd, …).
2. Count first-choice votes; if a candidate has >50%, they win.
3. Otherwise eliminate the lowest candidate and redistribute their ballots.
4. Repeat until a majority winner emerges.

## Key characteristics
- Reduces the spoiler effect and vote-splitting compared to [[Plurality]].
- Ensures the winner has majority support among remaining ballots.
- Requires ranked ballots and a more complex tabulation.
