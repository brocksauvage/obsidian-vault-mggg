# Plurality

**Plurality** (also called *first-past-the-post*) is a single-winner rule in which each voter casts one vote for one candidate, and the candidate with the most votes wins. A majority is **not** required—only more votes than any other candidate.

## How it works
- Each voter selects a single candidate.
- Votes are tallied; the highest total wins.

## Key characteristics
- Simple to administer and understand.
- Can elect a candidate opposed by a majority of voters when the field is split (the "spoiler" effect).
- Tends to favor two-party systems (Duverger's law).
- Used in most U.S. and U.K. single-member district elections.
